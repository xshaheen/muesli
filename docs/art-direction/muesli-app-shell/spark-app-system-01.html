The floating Mini and the meeting object now speak a settled visual language — warm charcoal glass, a coral–amber activity signal, continuous radii, 0.16 s morphs. The app window does not. This node carries that language inward, and reorganises settings around the objects people actually configure instead of the panes we happened to grow.

01 · The problem

Muesli currently ships two products with two identities

A user holds the hotkey, a warm coral disc appears under their caret, stretches into a charcoal glass capsule, resolves into an amber orb. Then they open the app to change the model — and land in a cool blue-black window with a blue accent and a segmented control. Nothing carries over.

Floating surfaces — settled

Warm charcoal #211f1e, coral #ff7043, amber #ffb04d. Inter. Radii 11 / 14 continuous. HUD material behind a 60 % tint, one hairline edge at 16 %. Motion: 0.16 s morph, 0.26 s pop, 0.14 s fade.

App shell — unrelated

Cool blue-black #111214, blue accent #6ba3f7, user-overridable to 7 presets. SF Pro via .system(). Radii 6 / 10 / 14 / 20, default corner curve. No shared motion vocabulary.

What is actually broken in settings

  • AI provider credentials are configured in four separate places. Dictation Cleanup, Meeting Summaries, Meeting Transcript Cleanup and Computer Use each carry their own Account / API Key / Model / URL rows for the same six providers. Paste an OpenAI key for dictation and meeting summaries still ask for one.
  • Language is configured in three places — Settings › Dictation languages, the Nemotron picker in the Models tab, and the Cohere language card in the Models tab.
  • Model download and model selection live in different tabs. You download in Models, then select in Settings › Speech Recognition, then choose the meeting final-transcript source in Settings › Meetings.
  • A segmented control does not scale to this. Six equal-weight panes, but Meetings holds 8 sections and ~25 rows while Sync holds 2. Every competitor moved to a settings sidebar years ago.
  • Appearance is a junk drawer — dark mode, menu-bar icon, hotkey display, accent colour, sound effects, the idle dot, next-meeting display and Marauder’s Map.
  • Four libraries are modal or orphaned. Writing Styles opens as a sheet from Settings, Meeting Templates as a sheet from Meetings, Cleanup Prompts as a sheet, and Insights has no sidebar entry at all — it is reachable only by clicking the stats header.

Thesis. The app window inherits Spark’s palette, geometry and motion — but never its material. Glass is the signature of a surface floating over someone else’s work; inside our own opaque window there is nothing behind it to blur, and faking it is the tell of an AI-generated interface. Same colours, same radii, same timings, opaque.

Five patterns to adopt

1 · Settings sidebar

Nobody with more than five sections uses a segmented control. Vertical, scannable, deep-linkable.

2 · Libraries in the sidebar

Dictionary, Styles, Templates, Prompts are content people curate. They stop being sheets.

3 · A credentials registry

Providers are configured once; features declare which provider they use. Nobody pastes a key twice.

4 · Named profiles

Superwhisper Modes, VoiceInk Power Mode, Wispr Style. Muesli already has per-app Writing Styles — it is a mode system that never got promoted.

5 · Privacy as a destination

Permissions, screen context, sync, retention and diagnostics belong on one page a privacy-conscious user can read top to bottom.

— and one to reject

Wispr’s Account / Billing group. Muesli is local-first and free; a billing shelf would be dead chrome.

Muesli’s unearned advantage. No competitor here does dictation and meetings and 11 on-device models seriously. That breadth is why the settings sprawled — and why a registry-plus-assignments model pays off more here than it would for any of them.

Open question, answered: the shell uses SF Pro (.system()) while the floating surfaces use bundled Inter (AppFonts). Recommendation is Inter everywhere in the app window, SF Pro left in place only where AppKit hands us a system control we do not draw. Inter is already bundled and registered, so this costs nothing; the alternative — moving the Mini to SF Pro — would undo settled visual QA on 22 pt chrome.

Geometry & motion

Radii — continuous curve everywhere

4 chip · 6 control · 10 field / row · 14 card / panel · 22 window. The 11 pt pill and 14 pt panel already ship; cornerCurve = .continuous becomes non-negotiable — today the app uses the default circular curve, which is the single most visible difference at a glance.

Motion — lifted verbatim

0.16 s morph · 0.26 s pop-in (0.55 → 1.06 → 1) · 0.14 s fade · 0.4 s hover grace. Tab switches, sheet presentation and row disclosure all take the 0.16 s morph. Reduce Motion collapses every one to the fade.

Rhythm

4 pt grid retained. 22 pt is the module — the Mini capsule height, the meeting row height, the sidebar item height, the control height. A settings row is 2×22 + padding.

Material rule

HUD glass belongs to floating panels only. In-window surfaces are opaque at the same hex. One exception: the window’s own titlebar may use the system material, because macOS owns it.

And I priced C wrong. I called it “a new persisted object… not a one-release change” from the mockup, before reading the style system. Having read it: DictationStyleGroup is already { id, name, styleID, matchers[] }, where a matcher is a bundle ID or a hostname — that is a Mode with a one-field payload. DictationStyleResolver.resolve already ships the precedence ladder (exact hostname exception → exact bundle exception → best hostname group → best bundle group → legacy domain/app rules → category), with normalisation, collision detection, quarantine on malformed config and a legacy projection. WritingStylesView is already the routing editor for it — its own subtitle reads “Choose a global style, reusable app groups, and exact exceptions.”

So C is not build a mode system. It is widen the payload a group carries from one styleID to a full Mode, and promote the routing editor that already exists from a modal sheet to the sidebar. That is materially cheaper than I told you, and it makes C the better call rather than merely the braver one.

Most of it already ships — under a smaller name

Choosing C makes this the load-bearing object, so it needs a real definition rather than a mockup. The good news from the source: the routing half is built, tested and shipping as “Writing Styles”. What C adds is payload and prominence.

They are two halves of one system: Writing Styles is the routing (which app gets which prompt), Cleanup Prompts is the bodies. Under C the routing half is promoted to Modes and stops being a library item at all. Library is Dictionary · Prompts · Templates — three, not four.

Anatomy — and what is inherited rather than copied

Every field below defaults to inherit. A Mode stores only what it overrides, so a new Mode is one matcher and one changed row, not a full configuration to fill in. This is what keeps the object from becoming the settings sprawl it is meant to fix.

FieldDefaultWhy it is per-mode
Matchers— requiredthe only field with no global fallback
Hotkeyinherit (the global dictation hotkey)a mode you want on demand rather than by context
ModelinheritParakeet for code, a multilingual model for chat — the reason 11 models exist
Languagesinherit the language profileEnglish-only in an IDE, code-switching in messages

Cleanup on/off + promptinheritthis is today’s styleID, unchanged
Cleanup providerinherit the assignmentreferences AI Providers — a Mode never holds a credential
App context / OCRinheriton in an editor, off in a password-adjacent app

This makes D5 more necessary, not less. If a Mode could hold its own API key, four duplicated credential blocks would become N duplicated credential blocks. A Mode assigns a provider by reference; the registry stays the single place a key is ever pasted.

Precedence — shipped, and now visible

1. exact exception on hostname ships today 2. exact exception on bundle ID ships today 3. best matching Mode by hostname ships today (group) 4. best matching Mode by bundle ID ships today (group) 5. legacy domain rule → app rule → category ships today, pre-ruleset configs 6. global defaults ships today added by C: an explicitly invoked Mode (its own hotkey, or picked from the Mini’s menu) wins over 1–6 for that one dictation and does not persist.

Hostname beats bundle ID at every tier, which is already the shipped behaviour and the right one: a browser is a container, the site is the context. The inspector states the winning tier in plain language (“active in Cursor — matched by app”), because the single largest complaint about mode systems is not knowing which one fired.

What stays global under C

C shrinks settings but does not empty them. Global keeps everything that is not per-context: launch and menu bar, microphone and audio session, the model library (downloads, updates, disk), the language profile that Modes inherit, the provider registry, meetings in full (a meeting has no focused app to match on), permissions and privacy, appearance, hotkeys, and diagnostics. What leaves global is the per-context half of Dictation — model, languages, cleanup, style, dictionary scope, app context — which is exactly the set that a Mode overrides.

Migration — the part to get right

  • Ruleset configs (dictation_style_ruleset_initialized = true): each group becomes a Mode with the same id, name and matchers; every other field is inherit. Behaviour is bit-identical on day one — a user who never opens Modes sees no change.
  • Pre-ruleset configs: the legacy app and domain rules keep resolving through tier 5 exactly as they do now. They are not force-migrated; the existing LegacyProjection already models this and should keep doing so.
  • Quarantine: malformed dictation_style_groups already falls back to empty with a stored reason. That path must survive the widening, and the reason should surface in Modes rather than only in config.
  • The trap: shipping Modes while leaving Writing Styles reachable would be two editors writing one ruleset. The sheet is removed in the same change that adds the sidebar, not after it.

2Presentation only — one AI Providers page that still writes to today’s four separate key sets, deduplicating the UI but not the storage.
Taken — 1, conditional on a tolerant config migration (read the four legacy key sets, write the registry, keep decoding both for one release — the pattern already used for show_dictation_focus_reminder → show_dictation_idle_dot). Option 2 keeps the “paste the key twice” bug alive under a nicer surface, which is the worst of both. If the migration turns out not to be tolerable, this drops to option 2 rather than shipping a lossy rewrite — and that is a decision I will bring back to you rather than take.

What this settles, and what it does not

Settled enough to plan against: the palette, geometry and motion (inherited, not invented), the Modes shell, the Mode object’s anatomy, inheritance and precedence, the migration from the shipped style ruleset, the nine settings sections and the row-by-row map. That is a complete implementation brief.

Deliberately not settled here: the onboarding flow, which consumes these surfaces and should follow rather than lead; and the meeting detail view’s document treatment (Granola’s “the note is the product” idea, recorded in the research as its own node).

The one thing I would not ship blind: Modes changes what happens when someone dictates, not just where a setting lives. The migration must be behaviour-identical on day one — a user who never opens Modes should not be able to tell the release happened. That is a testable property, and it is the acceptance bar I would hold the implementation to.
