Art direction · muesli-app-shell · node 01

Spark
one design system for the whole app

The floating Mini and the meeting object now speak a settled visual language — warm charcoal glass, a coral–amber activity signal, continuous radii, 0.16 s morphs. The app window does not. This node carries that language inward, and reorganises settings around the objects people actually configure instead of the panes we happened to grow.

01 · The problem

Muesli currently ships two products with two identities

A user holds the hotkey, a warm coral disc appears under their caret, stretches into a charcoal glass capsule, resolves into an amber orb. Then they open the app to change the model — and land in a cool blue-black window with a blue accent and a segmented control. Nothing carries over.

Floating surfaces — settled

Warm charcoal #211f1e, coral #ff7043, amber #ffb04d. Inter. Radii 11 / 14 continuous. HUD material behind a 60 % tint, one hairline edge at 16 %. Motion: 0.16 s morph, 0.26 s pop, 0.14 s fade.

App shell — unrelated

Cool blue-black #111214, blue accent #6ba3f7, user-overridable to 7 presets. SF Pro via .system(). Radii 6 / 10 / 14 / 20, default corner curve. No shared motion vocabulary.

What is actually broken in settings

  • AI provider credentials are configured in four separate places. Dictation Cleanup, Meeting Summaries, Meeting Transcript Cleanup and Computer Use each carry their own Account / API Key / Model / URL rows for the same six providers. Paste an OpenAI key for dictation and meeting summaries still ask for one.
  • Language is configured in three places — Settings › Dictation languages, the Nemotron picker in the Models tab, and the Cohere language card in the Models tab.
  • Model download and model selection live in different tabs. You download in Models, then select in Settings › Speech Recognition, then choose the meeting final-transcript source in Settings › Meetings.
  • A segmented control does not scale to this. Six equal-weight panes, but Meetings holds 8 sections and ~25 rows while Sync holds 2. Every competitor moved to a settings sidebar years ago.
  • Appearance is a junk drawer — dark mode, menu-bar icon, hotkey display, accent colour, sound effects, the idle dot, next-meeting display and Marauder’s Map.
  • Four libraries are modal or orphaned. Writing Styles opens as a sheet from Settings, Meeting Templates as a sheet from Meetings, Cleanup Prompts as a sheet, and Insights has no sidebar entry at all — it is reachable only by clicking the stats header.

Thesis. The app window inherits Spark’s palette, geometry and motion — but never its material. Glass is the signature of a surface floating over someone else’s work; inside our own opaque window there is nothing behind it to blur, and faking it is the tell of an AI-generated interface. Same colours, same radii, same timings, opaque.

Five patterns to adopt

1 · Settings sidebar

Nobody with more than five sections uses a segmented control. Vertical, scannable, deep-linkable.

2 · Libraries in the sidebar

Dictionary, Styles, Templates, Prompts are content people curate. They stop being sheets.

3 · A credentials registry

Providers are configured once; features declare which provider they use. Nobody pastes a key twice.

4 · Named profiles

Superwhisper Modes, VoiceInk Power Mode, Wispr Style. Muesli already has per-app Writing Styles — it is a mode system that never got promoted.

5 · Privacy as a destination

Permissions, screen context, sync, retention and diagnostics belong on one page a privacy-conscious user can read top to bottom.

— and one to reject

Wispr’s Account / Billing group. Muesli is local-first and free; a billing shelf would be dead chrome.

Muesli’s unearned advantage. No competitor here does dictation and meetings and 11 on-device models seriously. That breadth is why the settings sprawled — and why a registry-plus-assignments model pays off more here than it would for any of them.

Open question, answered: the shell uses SF Pro (.system()) while the floating surfaces use bundled Inter (AppFonts). Recommendation is Inter everywhere in the app window, SF Pro left in place only where AppKit hands us a system control we do not draw. Inter is already bundled and registered, so this costs nothing; the alternative — moving the Mini to SF Pro — would undo settled visual QA on 22 pt chrome.

Geometry & motion

Radii — continuous curve everywhere

4 chip · 6 control · 10 field / row · 14 card / panel · 22 window. The 11 pt pill and 14 pt panel already ship; cornerCurve = .continuous becomes non-negotiable — today the app uses the default circular curve, which is the single most visible difference at a glance.

Motion — lifted verbatim

0.16 s morph · 0.26 s pop-in (0.55 → 1.06 → 1) · 0.14 s fade · 0.4 s hover grace. Tab switches, sheet presentation and row disclosure all take the 0.16 s morph. Reduce Motion collapses every one to the fade.

Rhythm

4 pt grid retained. 22 pt is the module — the Mini capsule height, the meeting row height, the sidebar item height, the control height. A settings row is 2×22 + padding.

Material rule

HUD glass belongs to floating panels only. In-window surfaces are opaque at the same hex. One exception: the window’s own titlebar may use the system material, because macOS owns it.

05 · The contested decision

Where do settings live?

The palette is not contested — it is already shipped on the floating surfaces. What is genuinely open is the navigation model, and it is the choice that decides how far the reorganisation can go. Three variants, from conservative to ambitious.

A — Continuum · one window, settings nests in the sidebar

Keeps today’s single-window model. Settings stops being a segmented pane and becomes a sidebar section that expands in place, exactly like Wispr Flow’s Hub. Libraries are promoted out of their sheets. Insights finally gets an entry.

C — Modes · the profile becomes the object

The ambitious one. Muesli already has per-app Writing Styles — a mode system that was never promoted. Variant C makes a Mode the primary object: a named bundle of model, languages, cleanup provider, writing style, dictionary scope and hotkey, bound to apps or sites. Global settings shrink to what genuinely is global. This is Superwhisper’s Modes and VoiceInk’s Power Mode, done with Muesli’s on-device model range behind it.

Gains
Answers the question people actually have — “why did it write it that way here” — in one screen. Settings shrink dramatically because most rows are per-context, not global. It is the only variant that turns Muesli’s 11-model range into a visible advantage rather than a picker.
Costs
The largest change by far: a new persisted object, migration from DictationStyleResolver’s per-app rules, resolution precedence to design and test, and a real risk of ending up with two overlapping systems if the migration is partial. Not a one-release change.

Recommendation: B now, C next. B is the correct macOS shape and it is what unlocks the credentials registry — the single biggest usability win in this document — without betting the release on a new persisted object. C is the right two-releases-out destination, and B’s Connected / Used by page is exactly the layer a Mode later overrides. A is the fallback if a second window is judged too costly.

07 · Open decisions

Five calls that need your judgment, each with a lean

D1 — Which shell?
AContinuum — one window, settings nested in the sidebar. Cheapest; least native.
BWorkbench — content window plus a real ⌘, preferences window. Native; one more window to own.
CModes — the per-context profile becomes the primary object. Highest ceiling; largest migration.
Lean: B, with C as the next destination. B is what Mac users expect from ⌘, and it is enough to land the credentials registry. C should be planned, not attempted in the same release.
D2 — Does the user-selectable accent survive?
1Coral becomes the fixed product accent; the 7 presets are removed. Strongest identity, removes a shipped preference.
2Coral is the default; presets stay but are scoped to selection and highlight only — the record dot, the Mini and every semantic signal stay coral regardless.
3Status quo — the accent tints everything, and the Mini keeps its separate ownership.
Lean: 2. The Mini already owns its palette independently of recordingColorHex and that boundary is documented in the mini-indicator brief; option 2 makes the app follow the same rule instead of inventing a third. Removing a preference people already set (option 1) is a real regression for no design gain.
D3 — Inter or SF Pro in the app window?
1Inter everywhere — matches the floating surfaces; already bundled and registered by AppFonts.
2SF Pro in the window, Inter on the floating surfaces — most native, but keeps two identities.
Lean: 1, with SF Pro kept for AppKit-drawn system controls we do not render ourselves (menus, popup buttons). Mixing is invisible at those sizes; the headline type is what carries identity.
D4 — Does the light appearance go warm?
1Warm paper — #f7f4ef ground, warm greys, coral accent. One identity in both appearances.
2Keep neutral grey light mode; only dark mode goes warm.
Lean: 1. Half a redesign reads as a bug. The warm light ramp above is contrast-checked against the same ink ladder the app already uses.
D5 — How far does the credentials registry go in v1?
1Full registry — providers stored once, four assignment rows, migration from the four existing config key sets.
2Presentation only — one AI Providers page that still writes to today’s four separate key sets, deduplicating the UI but not the storage.
Lean: 1 if the config migration is tolerant (read old keys, write new, keep decoding both for a release). Option 2 keeps the “paste the key twice” bug alive under a nicer surface, which is the worst of both.

What I need from you

D1 and D5 change the shape of the work and are worth a real answer. D2, D3 and D4 have safe leans — if you say nothing I will build them as recommended. Mark up this page or reply with the letters.
